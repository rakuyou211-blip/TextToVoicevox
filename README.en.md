Turn PDFs, images, and text files into spoken audio with VOICEVOX, completely offline.

Hi. I'm rakuyou211, a solo hobby developer. I made this because I wanted my own machine to read things out loud to me — books, scanned documents, long PDFs — without sending anything to the cloud. It grew from there. It's still young and a little rough in places, and I try to be honest about that below.

> **Unofficial.** TextToVoicevox is not an official VOICEVOX product. It's an independent tool made by one person, not affiliated with or endorsed by the VOICEVOX project or the characters' rights holders. VOICEVOX itself is a separate program you install yourself — it isn't included.

## What it does

You give it a file. It pulls the text out, cleans it up, and reads it aloud in a VOICEVOX voice (Zundamon and others). You can also save the speech as an audio file.

- **Read from screen** — press **📷 スクリーンショット** (screenshot) and drag a box around any text on your screen (e-book, PDF viewer, web page) and it's read aloud immediately (Ctrl+R / ⌘R). After turning a page, the **同じ範囲を読む** button that appears after your first selection (Ctrl/⌘+Shift+R) reads the same spot again without re-selecting — or tick **めくったら自動で読む** (auto-read on page turn) and it reads each new page by itself as you flip through. Offline, like everything else; macOS needs the one-time *Screen Recording* permission.
- **Reads from many formats** — PDF (with a text layer, *or* scanned pages via OCR), images (PNG, JPG, BMP, TIFF, WEBP, GIF), and `.txt`, `.docx`, `.epub`.
- **No running heads in PDF books** — the book or chapter name printed at the top or bottom of each page and the page numbers are left out, so they aren't read on every page (a chapter-name head used to add a chapter pause and an M4B chapter on every page). The first occurrence of a chapter heading (第一章 and so on) stays; see what was taken out under **直した所** (fixes), and turn it off with **PDF のページの見出しと番号を読まない** under **設定 ▷** in section ①.
- **Offline OCR** — for scanned pages and images. Nothing is uploaded. Windows uses **PP-OCRv6** (through RapidOCR; both Apache-2.0): **it is installed automatically during first-time setup, so there is nothing to press** (about 122 MB download, about 100 MB on disk). It reads text over game scenes and photos, small text, and Japanese mixed with English; in our test of 258 images it read 99.7% of characters correctly, against 89% for the OCR built into Windows. If it could not be installed, or you delete the `ocr_engine` folder, the built-in **Windows.Media.Ocr** is used, and a **読み取りを強くする** button appears under **設定 ▷** in section ① to install it again. macOS uses **Apple Vision**. Vertical Japanese is read column by column, starting from the right.
- **Local text-to-speech** — speech is generated by **VOICEVOX** running on your own computer (see setup below). No online voice service.
- **Continuous playback with bookmarks** — start a long document, stop whenever (Space to pause/resume), and pick up from where you left off. A failed line is skipped, not fatal.
- **The line being read stays in view** — during continuous playback it is kept about a third of the way down the text, so the next sentence is visible and the end of a long wrapped sentence isn't hidden (if you scroll by hand, it waits 6 seconds before following again). The player bar shows the line number and the time left (あと約◯分).
- **Reading mode** — **◁ 広げる** (widen, F11) at the right end of the player bar folds the left column so the text fills the window, and the text grows with it. **▷ 戻す** (back), or Esc when nothing is playing, brings the column back.
- **Longer pauses between paragraphs and chapters** — where a new paragraph starts (after a blank line, or at a line indented with a full-width space) the pause is 2.5× the sentence gap, and before a chapter heading such as 第一章 or プロローグ it is 5× (1.0 s and 2.0 s with the default 0.4 s). It applies to playback, saved audio, subtitles and M4B chapter times; switch it with **段落・章は長め** in section ③ (on by default). Because the full-width-space indent at the start of a paragraph is kept and recognised, novels keep their paragraph pauses even with **空行を削除** (remove blank lines, on by default). For text without indents, untick it under **設定 ▷** in section ① so the blank lines between paragraphs are kept.
- **Audiobook export (M4B with chapters)** — pick **M4B(本)** as the format in section ③ to combine the whole book into one file with chapters auto-detected from headings like 第◯章 (or every ~10 minutes if there are none), ready for Apple Books.
- **Official voice samples** — audition each character/style with one click before committing to a voice.
- **The voice's face** — section ② shows the official illustration of the character and style you picked (each style has its own expression); click it to hear the voice sample. The images come from your own VOICEVOX — none are bundled with the app.
- **Reading check** — previews show *how* VOICEVOX read the line in kana, so misreadings jump out and go straight into the dictionary.
- **Furigana from VOICEVOX's own reading** — small kana appear above the kanji in the text, showing how VOICEVOX will actually read them (on by default), so you can catch misreadings before you listen. Readings that differ from the dictionary are colored — likely misreadings — and clicking one opens the pronunciation dictionary with that word filled in. Tick **誤読候補だけ** ("likely misreadings only") to show just the colored ones. In a long book, **次の候補 ▷** (next candidate, F8; Shift+F8 goes back) jumps from one likely misreading to the next, checking lines you haven't scrolled to yet as it goes. Furigana appear only while the app is connected to VOICEVOX; until then a line right above the text says so (ふりがな（漢字の上の読み）は、VOICEVOX を起動すると出ます — "furigana appear once VOICEVOX is running"), with a **画面なしで起動** button next to it that starts it.
- **Text that fits the window** — widen the window and the text (with its furigana) grows until a line holds about 36 characters; in a narrow window it never shrinks below your chosen size. Ctrl/⌘ + plus / minus (or Ctrl + mouse wheel) sets your own size and Ctrl/⌘+0 resets it; **文字の大きさ** (text size) in the right-click menu turns the automatic sizing on or off.
- **`#` memo lines** — lines starting with `#` are script notes: visible in the editor, excluded from narration, synthesis, and exports.
- **Cancellable everything** — extraction (even mid-OCR), synthesis (with "save what's done so far"), and previews can all be stopped.
- **Per-line voice switching** — start a line with `@ずんだもん:` to change the voice for that line, so you can give different voices to different characters or sections. Lines of dialogue (starting with 「) can also get their own voice under **お気に入り・セリフ ▷** (favorites and dialogue) in section ②.
- **Save audio** — export **WAV, M4A, or MP3**. By default everything goes into one file (**全部を1つに**); under **まとめ方** (how to split) in section ③ you can also make one file per line (**1行=1ファイル**), per number of lines (**行数ごと**) or per paragraph (**段落ごと**). If a save would make more than 300 files, the app asks first. Long texts are generated in parallel to save time. WAV always works. M4A / MP3 / M4B need nothing extra on Windows 10 or later: the app uses the encoder built into Windows (if **ffmpeg** is on your PATH, it is used instead). macOS builds M4A and M4B with the built-in `afconvert`, and needs ffmpeg only for MP3.
- **Subtitles** — produce a `.srt` subtitle file timed to the audio.
- **Pronunciation dictionary** — teach it how to read names or words it gets wrong, once, and it remembers. Edit entries in place and preview the registered reading.
- **Finished speech is kept, line by line** — fix one misread line and regenerate: every unchanged line is reused instantly instead of re-synthesized. See or clear what is kept with **声の保管…** (stored voice) in section ③.
- **Right-click menu** — preview the clicked line, register a selected word to the dictionary, switch the voice of a line (`@name:`), or comment out lines with `#`, right where you are.
- **Open output folder & copy credit** — the completion dialog opens the save location and copies the required `VOICEVOX:character` credit line in one click.
- **Autosave** — the text pane is saved every 60 seconds, so a crash won't eat your edits.
- **Replace and fix-ups** — **置き換え ▷** (replace) and saved replacements (**登録した置き換え**) fix common OCR mistakes before reading; **読み取りの間違いを直す** (fix reading mistakes) in section ① fixes look-alike characters such as 力⇄カ and 一⇄ー automatically.
- **Fixes the readings VOICEVOX gets wrong** — before speaking, the app rewrites the forms VOICEVOX misreads, without changing your text: `25℃` → 25度, `10:30` → 10時30分, `2026/11/7` → 2026年11月7日, `10〜20人` → 10から20人, `-5℃` → マイナス5度, `100m` → 100メートル, `①` → 1, `㈱` → 株式会社, `草www` → 草. It applies to playback, previews, saved audio, furigana and the VOICEVOX exports (.txt, .vvproj). Things VOICEVOX already reads right (iPhone, 1,280円, 50%, 5km) and forms that could be a date or a fraction (`1/3`) are left alone. On by default; switch it with **数字・日付・記号の読み方を直す** at the top of the **読み方辞書...** (pronunciation dictionary) window.
- **Smart line reflow** — for image OCR it uses each line's on-screen position (bounding box) to reliably rejoin sentences that were split across wrapped lines, while leaving headings, bullet lists and separate blocks intact — no false merges.
- **Ruby / furigana removal** — strips ruby markup from Aozora Bunko texts, and `漢字(かんじ)`-style ruby from web novels, so they read cleanly.
- **Screen-capture noise removal** — for screenshots of news programs or embedded social posts, it drops the on-screen overlay text (clock, channel logo, social handles, arrows, stray numbers) that isn't part of the article, keeping the body. Conservative by design (when in doubt, it keeps the line); on by default. Toggle it with the "画面キャプチャのノイズを除去" option in the GUI or `--denoise` / `--no-denoise` on the CLI.
- **On-screen label removal** — for news image OCR it also removes short Japanese labels that plain text can't tell from the article (station logo, program name, timestamp, category — e.g. "MBSニュース", "国内"), using each line's position and font size to judge that it isn't body text. macOS only, sharing the same on/off as noise removal above; conservative (it never drops article body).
- **Starts the engine with the app** — opening the app also starts the VOICEVOX speech engine (no editor window), without asking. Turn it off with **アプリと一緒に VOICEVOX を起動** (start VOICEVOX with the app) under **接続設定…** at the bottom left. If it stops while you are using it, the app starts it again by itself, and playback or saving carries on.
- **Uses the GPU when it can** — if your PC has a GPU and your VOICEVOX is the GPU build, the app starts VOICEVOX on the GPU, so making audio is much faster (about 4× on an RTX 5060 laptop: a 300,000-character book took about 22 minutes instead of 86). If it doesn't work, the app goes back to the CPU by itself. Turn it off with **GPU があれば使う** (use the GPU if there is one) under **接続設定…**.
- **Four themes, dark title bar** — light, dark, high-contrast and a green *zunda* theme; in dark mode the Windows title bar goes dark too.
- **Guide in four languages** — the **使い方** link at the top right opens a how-to page that ships with the app (English, 日本語, 繁體中文, 简体中文), picked to match your system language. It works offline.
- **Command line** — there's a CLI too, if you'd rather script it than click.

The current release is **v1.26.0**. ## Who this is for (accessibility)

I mostly think about ordinary people and families, not power users. If reading on a screen is tiring for you, this might help:

- **Tired eyes / long documents** — let the audio carry you instead of staring at the page.
- **Small or hard-to-read text** — scanned PDFs and photos of pages become spoken words.
- **Dyslexia and reading differences** — hearing text alongside (or instead of) reading it can make a real difference.

It's not a medical or professional accessibility tool — it's just a small thing that I hope makes some days a little easier.

## Before you start — please read this order

This app is the **last** of three pieces. You need all three, in this order. On Windows, the one-line installer below brings its own Python, so you don't have to install it. Everywhere else (the Mac, or the Windows zip) you do need Python — please don't expect a one-click app yet.

**① Install and start VOICEVOX**
VOICEVOX is a separate, free program that actually makes the voices. It is **not included** with this app — you download and install it yourself from [the official VOICEVOX site](https://voicevox.hiroshiba.jp/). After that there is nothing else to do: **the app starts its speech engine for you**. This app talks to VOICEVOX on your own machine, normally at `http://127.0.0.1:50021`; if VOICEVOX has had to move to another port, the app reads the file VOICEVOX writes about itself and follows it there. If VOICEVOX isn't running, no audio will come out — and the furigana above the kanji won't appear either.

To start it yourself, use the **接続 (connection)** area at the bottom left, the app has a **VOICEVOX起動** button, which opens VOICEVOX as usual (editor window and all, for when you want to touch things up there afterwards), and a **画面なしで起動** (start without the window) button, which starts only the speech engine without opening VOICEVOX's window — lighter and quicker, and it closes together with the app. Either way the app connects on its own once the engine is ready.

When you open the app and VOICEVOX isn't running, the app starts just the speech engine, without asking (no editor window; if VOICEVOX is already open, it connects to that; the engine closes together with the app). To turn this off, untick **アプリと一緒に VOICEVOX を起動** (start VOICEVOX with the app) under **接続設定…** (connection settings) at the bottom left.

**② Install Python**
(Skip this on Windows if you use the one-line installer below — it brings its own Python.) This app is a Python program. If you don't have Python yet, install it first (**Python 3.9 or newer** from the official python.org installer is recommended). On Windows, tick "Add Python to PATH" during install. The python.org installers for Windows and macOS already include tkinter, which the app's screen uses. On a Mac with Homebrew's Python, also run `brew install python-tk`; if the Mac setup can only find a Python without tkinter, it stops and tells you so.

**③ Install this app**
Download the release zip (below) and unzip it somewhere (right-click → *Extract All* on Windows, double-click on macOS — the OS built-in extractor is recommended; some third-party tools mangle Japanese filenames or drop execute permissions). Then:
- **Windows:** double-click **`起動.pyw`** — it is the only launcher in the zip. The extracted folder shows just that and the guides; the program and its parts are in the `app` folder. The first run opens a console window that sets things up, then opens the app; after that it starts straight away. After the first start, a **TextToVoicevox** shortcut with the app's icon appears in the same folder (Windows draws every `.pyw` file with the Python icon, so the launcher itself can't show the app's icon). Open that from then on; it does the same thing, even after you move the folder. 起動.pyw itself then moves into the `app` folder, so the top of the folder shows only the shortcut and the guides (if you delete the shortcut, open 起動.pyw inside `app` to make it again)
- **macOS:** don't double-click anything yet. Open Terminal, type `bash ` (with a trailing space), drag **`setup_mac.sh`** from the folder into the Terminal window and press Return. That gets past Gatekeeper and takes you from setup to launch; afterwards `起動.command` opens with a normal double-click

**④ Use it**
The window has two columns. On the left, work top to bottom: add files under **① 取り込む** (the text is extracted as soon as you add them; **▶ テキスト抽出 実行** extracts again), pick a voice under **② 声**, and set the output under **③ 保存**. On the right is the text; press **▶ 再生** right under it to listen, or **♪ 音声を生成** under ③ to save audio files. The buttons tell you what they are: the main actions — **▶ テキスト抽出 実行** (extract), **♪ 音声を生成** (make audio) and **▶ 再生** (play) — are **painted in the theme color** (orange in the light and dark themes, green in zunda, blue in high-contrast), buttons with a **plain filled face** are the everyday actions, and **outlined** ones are the occasional ones (saving the .txt, copying, previous / next line). Plain text is a tool, and the ones with a **gear** mark open **settings**; the red **本文を全消去** clears the text. If you get stuck, the **使い方** (how to use) link at the top right opens a how-to guide in your browser — in English, Japanese, Traditional or Simplified Chinese, matching your system language. In a narrow window, the furigana checkboxes move from above the text to below it.

That's it. The first run sets everything up automatically (it creates a local virtual environment and installs the dependencies — internet is needed only for that first run). After that, everything runs offline. You normally never touch the setup parts. To reinstall the libraries by hand, delete the `venv` folder (inside `app`) and open `起動.pyw` again on Windows, or run `setup.command` on macOS.

If macOS shows a security warning on that first double-click, see **First run** below — you only need to get past it **once**, and the launcher then clears the quarantine flag from the other files for you. On Windows, starting from `起動.pyw` means you should normally see no warning at all; **First run** below covers the rare SmartScreen prompt. The zip also ships with `はじめにお読みください.txt` (a "read me first" note, in Japanese) covering the same steps, and with four **`How to use (…).html`** pages — the same guide the **使い方** link opens, in English, Japanese, Traditional and Simplified Chinese; double-click one to read it.

If it won't start, run **`デバッグ起動.pyw`** (in the `app` folder; macOS: `デバッグ起動.command`) instead — it opens a console window so you can read the actual error. It works even when you have never managed to start the app at all. Two log files may also show up in the folder, and they mean different things: `起動エラー.log` is written only when the app fails to *start* (rewritten each time), while `エラー.log` records failures that happen once the window is open — extraction, synthesis, playback — appended as they occur and trimmed from the oldest end when it grows too big. If you report a problem, the matching log is the most useful thing to attach.

> Heads up: right now this is distributed as a **script zip**, not a ready-made `.exe` or `.app`. There is no packaged executable yet. That's on my list, but it isn't here today, and I'd rather say so than pretend otherwise.

## Windows: one-line install (recommended — no SmartScreen warning)

Open **Terminal** (right-click the Start button) or Windows PowerShell, paste this line and press Enter:

```powershell
irm https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rakuyou211-blip/TextToVoicevox/main/install.ps1 | iex
```

It downloads the build that has Python inside (the app, Python, its libraries and the image-text reader in one ~200 MB zip), puts it in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\TextToVoicevox`, creates a **TextToVoicevox** icon on the desktop and in the Start menu, and launches it — **you don't need Python on your PC**. That build is only offered through this line, not as a browser download: when a zip downloaded in a browser is extracted, Windows Defender holds Python's files for about four minutes, and the app can't open until then. Because PowerShell downloads the files itself, they never get the "downloaded from the internet" mark, so Windows doesn't show the *"Windows protected your PC"* warning. Run the same line again to update (your settings, dictionary and autosaved text are kept). When a new version is out, the app shows **新しい版があります** (a new version is out) at the top; choose **更新する** (update) and it swaps itself without pasting the line. It asks GitHub for the latest version number once a day, sending only the app's name and version; turn it off with **新しい版が出たら知らせる** under **接続設定…** (connection settings). No admin rights. The script is [`install.ps1`](install.ps1).

To uninstall, open Windows **Settings → Apps → Installed apps**, click **…** next to TextToVoicevox and choose **Uninstall**, or paste this line into PowerShell ([`uninstall.ps1`](uninstall.ps1)). Either way it removes that folder, the two icons and the Settings entry; VOICEVOX and the audio files you made are kept. If you installed v1.26.2 or earlier, run the install line once more to update and the app appears in Settings.

```powershell
irm https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rakuyou211-blip/TextToVoicevox/main/uninstall.ps1 | iex
```

## macOS: one-line install (recommended — no Gatekeeper warning)

Open **Terminal** (search "Terminal" in Launchpad), paste this line and press Return:

```bash
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rakuyou211-blip/TextToVoicevox/main/install.sh | bash
```

It looks for a usable Python (3.9+ with Tk 8.6) and, if there isn't one, offers to install a private Python 3.12 into `~/.local/python3.12` (no password needed). It puts the app in `~/Library/Application Support/TextToVoicevox`, installs its libraries, creates **TextToVoicevox.app** in `~/Applications` (so it shows up in Launchpad and Spotlight) and launches it. Because curl downloads the files, they never get the quarantine flag, so macOS doesn't say the app *"cannot be opened"*. Run the same line again to update. The first time you use 📷 *read from screen*, macOS asks for Screen Recording permission. To uninstall, trash that folder and the app. The script is [`install.sh`](install.sh).

## First run — if Windows or macOS warns you

A signed Windows launcher (`TextToVoicevox.exe`) is on the way: the certificate will come from the [SignPath Foundation](https://signpath.org/), with free code signing provided by [SignPath.io](https://about.signpath.io/), and the policy is in [docs/CODE_SIGNING.md](docs/CODE_SIGNING.md). **The application is still pending, so nothing is signed yet and the zip does not contain that `.exe`** — for now the app ships unsigned (`起動.pyw` does the launcher's job) — which is why your computer may show a scary-looking warning the first time. That warning is about the *lack of a signature*, not about anything malicious — but you should never just trust a stranger's word on that, so here's what's actually happening and how to check.

**You only need to get past a warning once, if at all.** Every launch clears the download flags (Windows' Mark of the Web / macOS' quarantine flag and lost execute permissions) from the rest of the files, so everything opens without warnings afterwards.

On **Windows**, `起動.pyw` removes the download mark from the files in its folder every time it runs — it just deletes the `Zone.Identifier` streams from Python, so it takes no noticeable time — and it also deletes the `.bat` files left behind by v1.23 and older if you extracted a new release over an old folder. On **macOS**, `起動.command` re-clears the quarantine flag and execute bit on the `.command` / `.sh` files in the folder on every run.

**Windows**

**Since v1.24.0 the Windows zip contains no `.bat` files at all.** Windows 11's **Smart App Control** blocks any `.bat` carrying the "downloaded from the internet" mark on the file extension alone — no contents check, no signature check, and no *Run anyway* button. Up to v1.23 the zip had both `起動.pyw` and `起動.bat`, and with file extensions hidden (the Windows default) both just read "起動"; whoever picked the `.bat` hit a dead end. `.py` / `.pyw` are not blocked, so the launchers are now `.pyw` only.

- If **"Windows protected your PC"** (SmartScreen) appears anyway: click the small **More info** → **Run anyway**, just this once.
- Extracting over an older version's folder is fine — `起動.pyw` removes the old `.bat` files. If the older version had everything at the top (v1.25 and earlier), it also moves your settings, voice cache and parts into `app` and removes the old program files (files you added yourself are kept).
- The one-line install above never shows a warning.

If you'd rather be careful first, that's completely reasonable.

**macOS — Gatekeeper**
You may see *"cannot be opened"* / *"Apple could not verify…"*. Two ways past it, either just this once:
- **The reliable way (any macOS, recommended):** open Terminal, type `bash ` (with a trailing space), drag **`setup_mac.sh`** from the folder onto the Terminal window, and press return. Running it through `bash` bypasses the quarantine block entirely — it also works when you get *"permission denied"* (some unzip tools drop the execute bit) — and repairs everything, then starts the app (running setup automatically on first use).
- **The mouse-only way (macOS 15 Sequoia and later):** close the warning with **Done**, then open **System Settings → Privacy & Security**, scroll down, and click **Open Anyway** next to the "起動.command was blocked" notice, then **Open**. (On macOS 14 and earlier, the classic right-click → **Open** → **Open** shortcut still works; macOS 15 removed it.)

**Antivirus false positives**
Unsigned scripts sometimes get flagged as "suspicious" even when they're harmless. That's a false positive from being new and unsigned, not proof of anything.

**How to actually trust it, instead of trusting me:**
- The full source code is right here in this repository — read it.
- It's open source under the **MIT license**, so anyone can inspect exactly what it does.
- It works **offline**; you can even disconnect from the internet after setup and watch it keep working.

If any of that gives you pause, please don't run it. I'd rather you stay safe than take my word for it.
